Friends, mats, mountains — the part that keeps the semester human

Table tennis

We restarted the Utah club together

The university club had gone quiet for years. Me and my friends decided to bring it back — refiled the paperwork, tracked down students who’d played about ten years earlier, begged for facility time, and slowly rebuilt the culture from zero. I was president for a while, but it was never a solo act: officers, coaches, volunteers, and regulars did the real heavy lifting.

Along the way we taught ESSF 1119 Table Tennis, grew to 150+ members, traveled to compete, hosted warm-up opens with 100+ players, and raised a bit of money for gear. I’m away at Penn now — the new officer team is carrying it forward and they’re awesome.

About the author

Short version for humans (and LinkedIn)

I grew up in Ukraine, finished undergrad at the University of Utah, and I’m now a master’s freshman in scientific computing at Penn. I care about problems where math meets something physical — blood flow in vessels, robots you can feel, GPU code that has to be fast.

I’m not trying to sound like a senior engineer with a decade of war stories. I’m a student who joins friends on half-baked ideas, enters contests, and keeps a research notebook messy on purpose. Hard things are usually just a bunch of small, doable steps — and a group chat that won’t let you quit.
