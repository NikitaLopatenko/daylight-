Table tennis

We restarted the Utah club together

The university club had gone quiet for years. Me and my friends decided to bring it back — we refiled the paperwork, tracked down students who’d played about ten years earlier, begged for facility time, and slowly rebuilt the culture from zero. I was president for a while, but it was never a solo act: officers, coaches, volunteers, and regulars did the real heavy lifting.

Along the way we taught ESSF 1119 Table Tennis, grew to 150+ members, traveled to compete (Weber State, Colorado Boulder, and more), hosted warm-up opens with 100+ players, and raised a bit of money for gear. I’m away at Penn now, but I hope to stop by whenever I’m back — the new officer team is carrying it forward and they’re awesome.

Peek at the club anytime: @uofutabletennis ↗ — feel free to stop by a practice if you’re at Utah.

About

Short version for humans and LinkedIn.

I grew up in Ukraine, finished undergrad at the University of Utah, and I’m now a master’s freshman in scientific computing at Penn (about two months in — still finding the good coffee). I care about problems where math meets something physical — blood flow in vessels, robots you can feel, GPU code that has to be fast.

I’m not trying to sound like a senior engineer with a decade of war stories. I’m a student who joins friends on half-baked ideas, enters contests, and keeps a research notebook messy on purpose. If this site says anything useful, it’s that hard things are usually just a bunch of small, doable steps (and a group chat that won’t let you quit).
